Lifecycle Architecture & Program Leadership

Global Nurture Program

$0 → $150M+ Pipeline in ~1 Year

I took a global nurture from a vision on a Miro board to $150M+ in pipeline in about a year, growing a single program into a family of interconnected, audience-specific programs built on one idea: a weekly-refreshing interest profile that routes each lead to the right nurture, stream, email, and language.

The Business Problem

Nurture was scattered across one-off programs that batch-blasted the same content to everyone, with no connected story. Nothing told us what each lead cared about, so performance was low. As the first program worked, more teams wanted in, each with its own audience, tone, languages, and business rules. One giant program couldn't handle that, and separate programs risked losing a consistent global experience.

The Goal

Replace scattered, batch-and-blast nurture with one global, interest-driven program that delivers each lead the most relevant content, and scale it across many audiences without losing a consistent global experience.

What I Did

I turned a vision sketched on a Miro board into a global Marketo nurture and ended up as its owner and builder. I designed each audience as its own engagement program, with streams of 4–5 emails, dynamic in 11 languages. Every email carried a Velocity-token promotional module with three layers of dynamic content: region, language, and informational versus engagement messaging.

A weekly-refreshing, propensity-based interest profile, which we pioneered, decided which nurture, stream, email, and language each lead received. It tracked each lead's interest in products and updated every week, so the content stayed relevant. A lead either moved to a different nurture or stream, or stayed where they were.

I built every program, email, token, and campaign, every quarter, and trained the team that took over.

Worked with: the global strategy lead and program owner (vision and strategy), the marketers behind each program (copy and goals), and the team I trained to run it after me.

Results

  • $150M+ in MQL pipeline in about a year
  • Audience grew from 1M to 9M leads
  • About 50% faster time to launch
  • Unsubscribe rate below industry average
  • 6 interconnected programs, 119 emails, dynamic in 11 languages
  • Other teams adopted the interest-profile approach for their own audiences
Custom Internal Tooling & Product Building

Global Nurture Hub

3 hrs → Under 30 min per Email

As more teams opted in, I taught myself to code alongside AI and built a web app that brought every marketer, builder, and stakeholder into one place, so I could run the current and upcoming quarters at once while onboarding a team.

The Business Problem

The program ran on spreadsheets, scattered Google Docs, and siloed conversations. Velocity tokens, Salesforce campaigns, and UTM strings were all built by hand, and change management lived in emails and chats. Nothing was tracked in one place and there was no consolidated reporting. Every new team meant duplicating the system, while I ran the program and onboarded a team at the same time.

The Goal

Give marketers, builders, and leadership one home for the whole program, replacing manual work and scattered tools, so it could scale across teams and quarters without duplicating the system.

What I Built

I taught myself to code alongside AI and built a web app that brought every marketer, builder, and stakeholder into one place.

For marketers: role-based workspaces so each team saw only its own nurture. They wrote copy in templates, sent themselves live tests by language, approved content, and tracked schedules and progress.

For the build team: one-click email builds in Marketo with correct branding, AI-assisted QA before and after each build, AI-assisted translation, localization import and export, change-request management, and generators for Velocity tokens, Salesforce campaigns, and UTM strings. It also tracked audience additions and suppressions, program transitions, pauses, and send-time changes.

For leadership: progress and launch-health dashboards for the current and upcoming quarter.

I then trained a new team of architects to take over day-to-day management.

Worked with: the director and the marketers who used it daily, and the team of architects I trained to take over.

Results

  • Build, QA, and approval for one email dropped from about 3 hours to under 30 minutes
  • One source of truth across 6 programs, 119 emails, dynamic in 11 languages
  • I could onboard and manage a team while running the program
Martech Implementation & Change Management

Knak Integration & Implementation

Architect-Dependent → Marketer Self-Serve

I implemented and integrated Knak to move email asset builds from an architect queue to marketer self-service, then led the training, documentation, and change management that made it stick.

The Business Problem

Email production ran through a multi-handoff legacy process. A marketer submitted a program intake form, and activation approved it or returned it for revisions. An architect then built the program and its assets, and the marketer and activation reviewed again before launch. Every asset waited in the architect queue, which slowed marketers and tied up architects with production work.

The Goal

Give marketers self-serve control of their own email assets, while architects kept ownership of program build and architecture.

What I Did

I partnered with the vendor and an implementation partner to implement and integrate Knak, then tailored it to our environment. That meant designing the modules, configuring user permissions, building the integration, and leading testing. I co-authored the documentation, led marketer training, and provided white-glove support through onboarding.

Change management: I was the first point of contact for every new Knak capability, dynamic content among them. For each, I walked teams through the benefits, implemented it, and onboarded them, so adoption kept pace with the platform.

Worked with: the vendor and an implementation partner (build-out and integration), activation (review), the marketers (training and onboarding), and colleagues on documentation.

Results

  • Marketers build and iterate on their own email assets without waiting on an architect
  • Architect capacity shifted from asset production to program architecture
  • A repeatable model of documentation, training, and onboarding for new teams and new features
Custom Internal Tooling & Marketo Integration

Live Email Preview App

Waiting on Test Sends → Instant Live Preview

I built a web app integrated with Marketo that lets marketers and activation preview, click through, and test any email on their own, with no coordination and no wait for a test send.

The Business Problem

When the “view as webpage” links were removed from emails, marketers lost their main way to preview emails and click through links. To see an email, they had to coordinate with activation or an architect, the people with Marketo access, to send a test and then watch an inbox for it. That created a communication handoff and a wait for every review.

The Goal

Give marketers and activation an instant, self-serve way to preview, click through, and test any email, without depending on Marketo access.

What I Did

I used AI-assisted coding in Google Apps Script to build a web app that integrates with Marketo. Enter a program ID and every asset in that program appears. Select an asset and a live preview opens beside it, where you can switch between HTML and text versions, choose a dynamic layer so the preview refreshes, and click through the CTAs. A single click sends a test to your own inbox.

I also built shareable deep links that save the exact search and email, so a link opens the right asset instead of the app’s home page. They carry the search data in UTM parameters, and activation could drop them into the program information worksheet, where preview links used to live.

Worked with: marketers and activation, who were both the users and the people I designed it with.

Results

  • Removed the coordination step between marketers and the people with Marketo access
  • Faster turnaround on previews and approvals
  • Less manual work for activation and architects
  • Marketers can find, preview, and test email assets on their own
Data Integration & Audience Strategy

Email Calendar & Audience Analytics

Fragmented Calendars → One Global View

I connected every regional email calendar into one global view and added audience overlap and comms limit analyzers, so teams could plan sends around the audience, not just the date.

The Business Problem

Each region kept its own marketing calendar, and nobody could see the whole picture. Marketers couldn’t see why an email’s actual audience came in so much lower than expected. The reason was often several programs competing for the same leads, with comms limits capping how many emails each lead could receive.

The Goal

Give marketers and activation one global view of every send, plus the audience data to plan around overlap and comms limits before an email goes out.

What I Did

I built a tool that brought every email calendar from Asana into one global view, covering global and regional programs together. To make that reliable, I put guardrails in place first. A sheet pulled campaign data from Asana nightly, and an AI agent corrected calendar tasks that had the wrong tags or names, so every campaign was captured. The calendar offers color coding, search, filtering, and weekly and monthly views.

Two analyzers sit on top of it, both pulling from Marketo. The audience overlap analyzer shows exactly how many leads overlap across any set of programs (A vs. B, B vs. C, C vs. A, and so on), working with smart lists or static lists. A marketer who sees three sends on one day can see how much those audiences overlap, because a lead in all three would receive only one.

For the comms limit analyzer, I built a master comms limit smart list in Marketo. Activation uses it on send day or 24 hours before to tell a marketer what share of their audience is hitting its limit, so they can decide to send on another day. Email notifications tell users when reports are ready.

Worked with: activation (planning and advising marketers) and marketers across regions (planning and insights).

Results

  • One global view of global and regional email programs
  • Fewer unsubscribes
  • Better send planning and scheduling clarity
  • Less audience exhaustion from over-mailing
